Anatomy of one nail

Six layers go on. Every one of them has to be safe to breathe.

A manicure is not one product. It is a stack, applied in order, and each layer is a separate decision about what goes near you for the next hour. Here is ours, taken apart.

  • Jojoba and vitamin E worked into the nail fold by hand. It is the last thing we do here and the first thing you do at home — the nail is dead, the skin that grows it is not.

    Free from Petroleum Synthetic fragrance Artificial dye

  • Gloss, and the seal that keeps the colour under it from lifting. A manicure that survives three weeks is a manicure you are not back in a chair re-doing — which is the cheapest way to reduce exposure to any of this.

    Free from Formaldehyde resin TPHP

  • The coat that sets the shade. Two thin passes cure more evenly than one thick one and hold longer, so the colour you leave with is the colour in the bottle.

    Free from Camphor Xylene Parabens

  • The layer the whole free-from list is really about. Polish is chosen off that list before it is chosen off a colour chart — the three named here are the ones every piece of guidance to pregnant women names first.

    Free from Formaldehyde Toluene DBP

  • Strength, built up in a soft gel and cured under light — not mixed in a dish from a liquid and a powder. That single choice is why this room has no monomer smell and no dust hanging in it.

    Free from MMA Liquid monomer Acrylic dust

  • The grip layer, and the one that decides how the nail underneath is treated. It bonds colour to the plate, so nothing has to be etched, filed thin or primed with acid to make the colour stay on.

    Free from Acid primer Heavy filing

If you are pregnant, or bringing a baby with you

Three things decide whether an hour here is a good hour. None of them is the colour.

  • What you breathe. No acrylic powder and no liquid monomer means there is no dust to sit in the air and nothing with a smell strong enough to make you want the door open.
  • What touches you. Waterless — no shared soaking bowl, and no cuticle cut down to skin that has to heal afterwards.
  • How long you sit. Six layers is a short appointment, and you can stand up between any two of them. Tell us and we stop.
